# Why not the other orals

I need this page so I do not borrow a neighbour's thesis in
the hallway.

## Not a saw

A change-point saw scores
`||μ_left − μ_right|| √(k(n−k)/n)` and recurses. It is a
good claim: a document is a walk, a change is a split. I
chose votes instead because I want to *name* the maps that
moved. A saw gives me a cut. An atlas gives me a cut and a
list.

## Not NCD

Normalised compression distance is a beautiful sentence:
same author, shared regularities, cheaper concatenation. On
paragraphs this short, zlib does not have regularities. I
will not spend the oral apologising for a saturated bit
cost.

## Not a blended register peak

A peak-picker on a single distance is `k=1` with extra
geometry. It will fire Flint's digit wrinkle if that wrinkle
is the loudest thing in a quiet trap. The bundle is there to
refuse that.

## Not logistic regression

I can fit a hinge classifier on pairs. With 92 hinges I can
overfit the bank and call it machine learning. I would then
owe regularisation, a split, and a story about coefficients
that are just these channels again. Votes keep the story
short.

## What I will concede

If the examiner wants a neural follow-up, I will say: mask
the nouns, train a pair encoder, keep a calibration set that
includes traps. I will not lead with that. The atlas is the
thing I can finish on paper.
