# Topic is not style

PAN spent 2023–2025 making this the official difficulty knob. Easy
sets let topic and author jump together. Hard sets hold the topic
still. I copied the knob with original text.

## Bands in this bank

| band | ids | topic | house |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| control | 01–04 | stays | stays |
| easy | 05–10 | jumps | jumps |
| medium | 11–15 | stays | jumps |
| hard | 16–19 | stays | jumps, closer houses |
| trap | 20–23 | jumps | stays |
| return | 24–26 | stays | ABA |
| collage | 27–28 | stays or jumps | three or four houses |

The trap band is the one I want on the oral table. Document 20 is
Caliper describing a stilling well and then Caliper describing a
cinema booth. A content model fires. Kerf does not.

Document 23 (holdout) is Seminar on a compass swing and then
Seminar on grain-elevator dust. The nouns are from different
centuries. The hedges are the same.

## Hard is not "I used a transformer"

Hard in this bank means the two houses share a register: Caliper
versus Seminar (both formal, few *you*), Pocket versus Bench (both
first person), Statute versus Seminar (both long). If I can still
cut those, the fingerprint is doing work. If I cannot, I say so.

## What I will not claim

I will not claim that closed-class features beat a fine-tuned
encoder on PAN hard. They do not have to. They have to be a
method I can compute on paper and a failure I can classify.
