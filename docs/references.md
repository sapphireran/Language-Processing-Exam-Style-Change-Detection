# References

Sources I actually opened or already knew well enough to cite in
an oral. Not a dump of every stylometry paper.

## Task overviews

- Zangerle, E. et al. Overview papers for the PAN multi-author
  writing style analysis task (2023, 2024, 2025). Difficulty by
  topical homogeneity; paragraph-level then sentence-level
  editions. <https://pan.webis.de/>
- PAN CLEF 2025 task page, *Multi-Author Writing Style Analysis*.
  Sentence-level change labels; three difficulty bands.

## Change-point and distance

- Killick, R., Fearnhead, P., and Eckley, I. A. (2012). Optimal
  detection of changepoints with a linear computational cost
  (PELT). *JASA*. The grown-up version of binary segmentation.
- Cilibrasi, R. and Vitányi, P. (2005). Clustering by compression.
  *IEEE TIT*. NCD. I use it as a foil, not as the saw.
- Hellinger, E. (1909). Neue Begründung der Theorie
  quadratischer Formen… The rate-space distance in
  `kerf.distance.hellinger`.

## Stylometry instincts

- Mosteller, F. and Wallace, D. L. (1964). *Inference and
  Disputed Authorship: The Federalist*. Function words as
  authorship cues.
- Argamon, S. and Koppel, M. (various). Closed-class and
  complexity features versus content.

## What I did not cite because I did not use it

Fine-tuned encoder baselines from recent PAN participant papers.
