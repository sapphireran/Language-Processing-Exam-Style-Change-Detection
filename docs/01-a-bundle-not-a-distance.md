# A bundle, not a distance

Dialect geography does not draw a new dialect because one village
says *bairn* and the next says *child*. It draws a line when a
*bundle* of isoglosses runs together: pronouns, vowels, a negation,
a habit of the verb. One line is a wrinkle. A bundle is a border.

Intrinsic style-change detection is the same claim in a smaller
room. A document is a short walk across a dialect atlas whose
axes are closed-class rates. I do not average those axes into one
number and then argue with a threshold. I ask, at each hinge,
**how many maps agree that a border is here**.

That is the whole thesis. I can defend it without a neural net.

## What I am not doing

I am not doing pairwise cosine on a blended register vector. A
blend lets a loud digit channel drag the hinge, or lets three
quiet person channels cancel. I am not doing global binary
segmentation on a concatenated mean. A global saw is a good
oral (it treats a change as a change-point), but it is a
different claim: one cut that maximises a mean shift. I am
doing **local votes**.

I am not doing compression distance. On eighty-word original
paragraphs zlib saturates. I would rather not spend the oral
explaining why my headline feature is a brick.

## What I owe the examiner

I owe a list of channels I can write from memory. I owe a
formula for a mark. I owe a reason `k=3` is not magic (one
feature is a wrinkle, two is a coincidence I still do not
trust, three is a bundle I will defend). I owe a trap: same
house, new topic, no fire.

If I cannot say which channels should flip at a hinge, I have
not finished writing the document.
