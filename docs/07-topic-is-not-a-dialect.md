# Topic is not a dialect

The easy band of the shared task lets topic and author move
together. A student who vectorises nouns will look brilliant
and will not know why. The hard band, and my traps, are there
to take that toy away.

## What a trap looks like

Document 25: Skiff on an eel weir, then Skiff in a hop garden.
The nouns fall off a cliff. The map does not. *I*, *I'll*,
*don't*, *so*, *then* stay. A noun model fires. A bundle
must not.

Document 27: Flint cider press, then Flint charcoal clamp.
Digits stay. Person stays at zero. The craft changes; the
hand does not.

Document 26 (holdout): Roll on a lime kiln, then Roll on a
millrace. Minutes stay minutes.

## What a hard file looks like

Document 19: Skiff and Vellum on the **same** eel weir. If I
fire, I fired because *I* left and *we* / *however* arrived,
not because the eels changed their mind.

Document 21: Twine and Brine on the same kiln. *You* and
*maybe* leave; *shall* arrives.

## The sentence I want in the oral

"I can change the aboutness of a paragraph without changing
its dialect, and I can change the dialect without changing
the aboutness. Only the second is a style change. My features
are the ones that cannot see the first."

If the examiner asks why I did not use a transformer, I say:
a transformer will eat the nouns unless I starve it. I can
starve a feature list by construction. I cannot starve a
pretrained encoder in ten minutes on a whiteboard.
