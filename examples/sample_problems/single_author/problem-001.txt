The pothos in the kitchen window has decided to colonize the spice rack. I keep meaning to trim it and then I get attached to the newest leaf, which is a ridiculous reason to live with a vine in the oregano. Watering is still a guess. I poke the soil, shrug, and add a little. It has survived worse management than mine, including the week I left for a conference and came home to a plant that looked personally offended.

I moved the snake plant to the hallway because it was judging the living room. That is not a metaphor I can defend. The hallway gets less light, which textbooks say it can handle, and so far the textbooks are winning. I wipe the dust off the leaves when I remember, which is not a schedule. If a plant can be low-maintenance, this one is trying to teach me the meaning of the phrase.

The little succulent on the desk is a lost cause in slow motion. I overwatered it out of guilt, then underwatered it out of penance. There is a wrinkled leaf that I cannot bring myself to pull. I rotate the pot a quarter turn every few days as if geometry were going to save it. Friends say I should start over. I say I am collecting data. The plant, if it could speak, would ask to be left out of the study.
