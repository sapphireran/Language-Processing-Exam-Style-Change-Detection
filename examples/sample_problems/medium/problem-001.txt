I keep saying I bake bread to relax and then I spend an hour staring at dough like it owes me rent. Today the starter was lively, which made me cocky, and cocky is how you forget the salt. The kitchen smelled right by late morning. Neighbors knocked because they always knock when the oven is on. I told them it might be a brick. They said they would eat a brick if it was warm.

The loaf cracked along the score the way I like, not the way the internet likes. I still slashed too timidly. Crust sang for a minute, then went quiet, and I did the thing where I tap the bottom and pretend I know what a hollow sound means. Butter went on while it was still too hot. I burned my tongue and called that research. If this one stales by Tuesday I will make croutons and claim that was the plan.

Dough strength is easier to discuss once gluten development is separated from hydration. At seventy-four percent water, this flour needed a short autolyse and two coil folds before the windowpane held without tearing. Bulk fermentation at twenty-four degrees reached a thirty percent rise in three hours. A colder bulk would have bought time, but it would also have flattened the acid profile I wanted for a thinner crust.

Final proof in a lined banneton went seventy minutes, which was slightly long for the room temperature. Oven spring was adequate rather than dramatic, consistent with a dough that had already spent much of its gas in the tin-free bulk. Crumb openness tracked the fold schedule more than the steam period. For a repeatable bake, I would lock hydration first and only then chase a darker Maillard finish by extending the uncovered stage.
