I want the boat to leave when it says it will, and I want the tannoy to stop calling it a "slight delay" when we're sitting there for twenty minutes watching gulls steal chips.

Also, bring a real jacket. I wore the denim one again like an idiot. My hands were useless by the time we hit the slip, and I dropped my travel card under a bench. A stranger fished it out with a glove I would not have touched. I said thanks too fast and then stared at the water like that was a personality. Seasonal inundation of brackish marsh platforms is increasingly out of phase with the phenology of dominant graminoids. Empirical water-level records indicate that high-water events now persist into the early growing season, with measurable implications for root-zone aeration and subsequent stem density. Attribution analyses further suggest that a combination of mean sea-level rise and altered wind setup accounts for a substantial fraction of the observed change in hydroperiod. These findings motivate tighter coupling between marsh-migration models and updated tidal datums rather than continued reliance on historical inundation climatologies.

Management responses that treat peak-season flooding as a residual outcome, rather than as a design constraint, systematically under-prepare restoration plantings. Scenario ensembles that hold sediment supply fixed while advancing the onset of prolonged inundation produce repeated failures of transplant establishment on the marsh interior. The literature is not unanimous on the magnitude of the phenological mismatch, yet the direction of the signal is consistent across independent gauge networks. Policy instruments that ignore that direction will allocate planting windows to weeks in which soils are already anaerobic.

Cast on eighty stitches in a wool that still has some bite. Do not use a slippery acrylic if you want the rib to stand up on a harbour walk. Work k2, p2 for eight centimetres, then switch to stocking stitch and keep the edges tidy with a slipped first stitch. Measure after a wash, not before. If the fabric pulls in, you went down a needle size too far; if it flares, you were timid at the hip shaping. Block it on a towel, not on a radiator.

Join the shoulders with a three-needle bind-off facing the right sides together. Pick up stitches around the neck in a ratio that does not strangle you — three stitches for every four along the sloped edge is a decent starting guess. Work a short rib, bind off in pattern, and weave the ends in the same direction the stitches travel. Sew the side seams with mattress stitch, tug once, and stop. A scarf that fights your coat is a failed scarf, however even the tension looks on the table.
