Glacier-wide mass-balance estimates derived solely from a handful of index stakes are sensitive to the representativeness of those stakes with respect to the hypsometry of the ice body. Where ablation is concentrated on a low-elevation tongue, an over-weighting of high-elevation accumulation measurements produces a systematically positive bias in the specific balance. Recent work therefore combines stake networks with repeat DEM differencing, using the geodetic result as a constraint on the glaciological interpolation. The residual between the two methods remains informative: it encodes density-assumption error and the treatment of internal accumulation.

Furthermore, seasonal timing of surveys is not a clerical detail. A spring-to-spring interval that truncates a late snowfall will attribute that mass to the following year, distorting trend detection in short records. The recommended practice is to report both fixed-date and stratigraphic balances, together with an explicit statement of the density profile used to convert volume change to mass. Without that statement, later syntheses cannot homogenise the series. These requirements are modest relative to the cost of maintaining a stake network, yet they are still omitted from a non-trivial fraction of published glacier reports.
