We ought to see a hurdle as a portable hedge, not as a rustic decoration. One therefore asks how the weave takes wind and how the feet take mud. However pretty a tight pattern looks, the claim is whether a ewe can lean. This does not require a catalogue.

Our hands remember the twist better than our notes. We should still write the notes. Therefore a day in the yard is both craft and record. One does not keep a skill by watching it from the gate.

A hurdle shall be woven to the stated width and height. The sails must be of the named year's growth. No broken rod shall be hidden in the body. The inspector shall reject a frame that springs.

Feet shall be bound before the first sail is started. The finished hurdle must stand without a wobble. Water shall not be used to force a dry rod. The stamp shall be placed on the top sail.
