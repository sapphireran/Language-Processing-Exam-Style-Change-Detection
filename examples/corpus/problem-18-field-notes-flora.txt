Site 4, west ditch, 07:40. Sky flat. Wind off the water. Caltha palustris in flower along the wet margin, at least forty clumps, some already going to seed. Note: boots not tall enough. Water in left sock. Photo 14–19.

Visited the willow at the bend. Old nest, empty. Two warblers heard, not seen. Collected one leaf with rust; bag 4C. Did not collect the pretty one because I could not name it and I am trying to be better than a magpie.

Caltha palustris L. occupies eutrophic wetland margins and is readily identified by its glossy reniform leaves and yellow sepals. Flowering in this latitude typically begins in April. Seed set proceeds while later umbels are still opening, which matches the mixed phenology recorded at Site 4.

Specimens should not be taken without a determination. The rust on Salix is likely Melampsora and is of less floristic interest than the unvouchered leaf the observer left behind. Future visits require taller boots; the sock is not a method.
