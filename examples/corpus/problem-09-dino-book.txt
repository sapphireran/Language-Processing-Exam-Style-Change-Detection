This is Rex. Rex has big teeth and small hands. Rex likes to stomp. Stomp, stomp, stomp! When Rex is hungry, Rex looks for a snack that runs. Running snacks are fast. Rex is faster. The end is not the end, because tomorrow Rex will stomp again.

See the nest? The nest has eggs. Eggs are not for stomping. Eggs are for waiting. The little ones will push, push, push, and then there will be more feet. Can you count the feet? Too many feet. That is a good problem.

Late Cretaceous deposits from the Hell Creek Formation preserve an assemblage in which tyrannosaurid remains are comparatively rare relative to hadrosaur and ceratopsian elements. Tooth-marked bone is nevertheless frequent, which is consistent with scavenging as well as predation. Body-size reconstructions that begin from isolated femora should be treated as provisional.

Nesting traces attributed to non-avian dinosaurs require taphonomic caution. Trampling, vegetation casts, and later burrows can mimic clutch geometry. Where eggshell microstructure is preserved, it supplies a stronger taxonomic argument than the silhouette in a children's plate. The popular stomp is a locomotion, not a phylogeny.
