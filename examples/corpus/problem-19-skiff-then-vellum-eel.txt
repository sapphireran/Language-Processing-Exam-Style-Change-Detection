I know this bend by the way it pulls. I don't need the map. I'll reset the bag after the lift and I'll check the mouth for sticks. So I keep my lamp low.

I lost a glove in the mud last night. I don't go after it. I'll cut a new pair from the spare hide and I'll oil them. Then I go back to the wing.

We ought to read a weir as a negotiation with a tide, not as a trap that happens to work. One therefore watches the slack as carefully as the run. However local the stakes look, the argument is the whole bend. This does not make a romance of mud.

Our records treat the catch as the only fact. We should refuse that. Therefore a ruined wing is as informative as a full bag. One does not understand a weir by counting eels.
