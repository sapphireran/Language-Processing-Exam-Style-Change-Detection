Came in early to cue the Sunday folk hour and the kettle in the kitchen still had Tuesday's tea in it, which is how I know nobody else loves this place the way they claim to. I talked too fast through the first song because a listener had emailed about the harbour bells and I got excited. That's not professional. I also don't care. If you're hearing this, the bells are at 11, the weather is doing its usual coastal shrug, and I will play the long live cut even if the board op sighs at me.

Brought biscuits. Left them by the mic so I'd actually eat. The request line rang once and it was someone asking whether we still do the lost-cat notices. We do. We always do. I said that on air and then played a waltz that has nothing to do with cats. The show is allowed to be a little stupid. That's the point of community radio, or at least that's the story I tell when the playlist looks like a jumble sale.

Meter the desk so the compressor is not doing all of the work. A hot vocal with a sleepy bed will still clip the transmitter if the output VU is ignored. Set the processing chain to the weekday preset before the news insert; the weekend preset carves the midrange in a way that makes speech lispy on small radios. Log the transmitter forward power at the top of each hour. If reflected power creeps, stop the show, not later. Keep the spare mic in the labelled case, not on the sofa where the biscuits live.

Do not route the request phone through the air chain without the dump delay armed. That is not a style preference. Check the emergency script is in the left drawer, paper, not a file on a machine that will be busy. Replace the cue-speaker cone when it rattles; you cannot hear a phase issue through a rattle. Record the playlist as played, including the waltz that was not on the sheet. The archive is the station's memory. A cheerful presenter is not a substitute for a completed log.

Presenters shall arrive twenty minutes before their appointed hour. They shall not treat the kitchen as a pantry for other people's tins. Lost-pet notices are permitted only at natural breaks and must include a contact that is not the presenter's personal number unless the presenter has agreed in writing. Live requests may be read only after the dump delay is confirmed armed. The Sunday folk hour remains a music programme; spoken segments shall not exceed four minutes without the board operator's assent. Biscuits are tolerated. Stale tea is not a filing system.

The station board may suspend a presenter who repeatedly ignores transmitter-log requirements. Appeals shall be submitted on paper to the volunteer secretary within ten days. This policy does not prevent a waltz. It prevents an unlogged waltz after an unlogged power reading. Courtesy on air is expected; courtesy is not a defence against a missing log. The request line is a public service, not a private chat, and shall be documented in the programme file.
