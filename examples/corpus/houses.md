# House voices

These six voices are the only authors in this toy bank. They are not
real people and they are not scraped. Each house is a closed-class
habit I can keep still while I rotate the topic.

The detector is not allowed to read the house names. They exist so I
can write documents on purpose, and so I can explain a miss without
waving at "the vibe".

An isogloss is a line on a dialect map where one feature flips. A
*bundle* of isoglosses is a change of hand. Nouns never enter the
map, so a Skiff paragraph about an eel weir and a Skiff paragraph
about a hop oast should look like the same dialect.

## Skiff

First person, contractions, short steps. Talks with the hands already
on the work. Favourite moves: *I*, *I'll*, *don't*, *my*, *so*,
*then*. Will not say *shall*, *however*, or *you*.

**Closed-class tell:** high *I* / contraction / oral *so*–*then*.
Almost no *the*-stacking. No digits as a habit.

## Roll

Committee minutes. Third person, past copula, *shall* / *must*, no
contractions. Sentences sit around twenty words. Favourite furniture:
*the committee*, *members*, *it was resolved*, *the clerk*.

**Closed-class tell:** *the*, *was* / *were*, moderate *shall*, zero
*I* / *you*, zero apostrophes.

## Twine

Second person advice with hedges. The reader is already holding the
tool. Favourite moves: *you*, *your*, *you'll*, *don't*, *maybe*,
*sort of*, *a bit*. Asks a question when the card is unsure. Will not
say *shall* or *however*.

**Closed-class tell:** *you* / *your*, hedges, contractions,
questions. No first-person essay *we*.

## Vellum

First person plural essay. Long sentences that refuse to land until a
caveat has been paid. Favourite moves: *we*, *one*, *our*, *ought*,
*however*, *therefore*, *this does not imply*. No contractions.

**Closed-class tell:** *we* / *one*, formal connectives, long
*n_words*, zero apostrophes, almost no *you*.

## Flint

Field card. No person. Numerals do the work. Fragments are allowed.
Favourite furniture: a time, a length, a count, a nil leak.

**Closed-class tell:** digit rate, tiny *n_words* per line of thought,
zero pronouns, zero contractions, zero hedges.

## Brine

Statute voice. *A X shall…* / *Y must not…*. Passive, no person, no
apostrophes, no *it was resolved*. The inspector exists only as a
role.

**Closed-class tell:** high *shall* / *must*, almost no *was* / *were*
(that is Roll's past meeting), zero *I* / *you* / *we*, zero
contractions.

## Why these six

They are six different dialect maps, not six topics. Skiff and Twine
both contract; they disagree on *I* versus *you* and on hedges. Roll
and Brine both deontic; they disagree on *was* versus a pure *shall*.
Flint is the only house that writes numbers as digits on purpose.
Vellum is the only house that pays *however* as rent on every page.

If I cannot say which channels should flip at a hinge, I have not
finished writing the document.
