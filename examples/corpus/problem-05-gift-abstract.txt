We present a compact encoder for nocturnal moth wing-beat audio. The model is trained on a weakly labelled set of garden recordings and a smaller expert set collected at a mercury-vapour sheet. We show that a two-second window is sufficient for family-level decisions when the background is dominated by a single lamp, and we report macro F1 on a held-out week in August.

The architecture follows a conventional convolutional stack with a temporal pooling head. Data augmentation is limited to gain jitter and mild time stretch; we avoid pitch shift because it collides with species-specific frequencies. An ablation without the expert set drops family-level F1 by a wide margin, which suggests that the garden labels are useful only as a pretraining soup.

I put the moths in because I already had the tapes and because the garden committee wanted a poster that was not another bike-lane map. The network is basically last year's bird model with the spectrogram window shortened. I did not invent a compact encoder. I changed a number and asked for the abstract to sound like we meant it.

Limitations include rain, overlapping wing beats, and a lamp that attracts a non-random sample of the local fauna.
