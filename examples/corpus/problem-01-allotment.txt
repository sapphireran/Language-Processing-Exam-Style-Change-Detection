I took the last Saturday of March as a truce with the allotment. The soil was still cold under the fork, but the bindweed had already started its optimistic crawl toward the beans I had not even planted. I worked from the back path so I could see the whole rectangle at once, which is the only way I ever decide what to sacrifice.

The compost heap had slumped into a wet shoulder. I turned it anyway, because last year I waited for a nicer day and then never did. A robin used the handle of the fork as a viewing platform and did not pay rent. I told it the plot was not a café. It stayed.

By noon I had a trench for the potatoes and a row of string that listed slightly toward next door's shed. I could have reset the string. I ate an apple on the upturned bucket instead and watched a neighbour argue quietly with a watering can.

The rain arrived as a rumour and then as a fact. I shoved the fork under the tarp, washed my hands in the water butt, and walked home with mud on a cuff I had promised to keep clean. The tomatoes can wait a week. The bindweed will not.
