I keep the king in front of the pawn until the knight has no safe check, then I edge forward one square at a time.
If they check from the side I step in, not away, because away is how you donate a tempo you will want later.
The draw is in the knight's schedule; I do not hunt it for sport.
Bring the king to the promotion square's neighbourhood before you push, or the knight will sit on the pawn's head.
You can afford one waiting move if the knight is offside; you cannot afford a cute check that lets it back in.
Tablebases call this a draw with perfect play, which is a sentence for engines and a warning for people.
Trade the cute check for a king move toward the square you actually need.
Then stop annotating and play the move you already named.
If the knight returns, repeat the same short plan; do not invent a second plan out of pride.
