The lighthouse on the headland is not romantic when you have to paint it. The salt eats the lowest band first, so we start there with scrapers and a wind that thinks it is funny. Gulls nest in the lantern deck and leave a white argument on every railing.

I learned to time the primer between squalls. If the metal is still cold to the palm, the paint sits on top like a lie and peels before August. The keepers who used to live here would have known that in their sleep. I only know it because last year's hatch blistered and I had to climb the stair twice.

Paper prefers dry hands and a table that does not rock. The first valley fold should be sharp enough to catch a fingernail. If the crease wanders, the crane's wing will remember the wander forever and you will call it character when you mean error.

A bird from a square is a small obedience. You reverse the sink fold, flatten the diamond, and pull the neck until the paper almost complains. I do not think about cliffs while I do this. I think about whether the head is too long, and whether I still have a second sheet if I am wrong.
