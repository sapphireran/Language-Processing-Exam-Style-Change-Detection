I don't trust a stake that won't take a twist. I'll walk the weir at slack and I'll feel for the ones that have gone soft. My hands know the mud better than any chart. So I splice the broken withy and I get out before the flood.

I set the wings tighter than last week. I don't like the gap at the third pair. I'll drive one more stake and I'll lash it down with the wet cord. Then I sit on the bank and I wait for the run.

I hauled a thin catch at first light. I don't mind a thin morning if the mouths are clean. I'll ice them in the box and I'll keep the slime off my knife. So I rinse the boards and I start the next lift.

I won't leave the old net in the current. I'll roll it, I'll pick the sticks out, and I'll hang it where the wind can work. My shoulder is loud about it. Then I lock the hut and I walk the bank path home.
