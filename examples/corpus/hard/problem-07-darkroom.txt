I mixed a fresh tray of print developer tonight because yesterday's had that tired tea colour I always ignore for one session too long. The first test strip on the harbour negative told me I'd been printing it too hard. I dropped a grade and the sky stopped looking like a cut-out. I still dodged the crane with a bit of card. I always dodge the crane. The safelight buzzes. I don't mind the buzz. It means the room is working and I'm not in the kitchen pretending this is a hobby I could do on a laptop.

Fixer was still fine. I checked it with the film-leader scrap I keep in a tin, which is a habit I picked up from someone who would hate how messy my wet side is. I don't squeegee prints anymore. I just let them drip. I lost a corner once to a cheap blade and I can still see it. If the tram noise comes through the wall I wait, because vibration on the enlarger column is real even when people laugh at that sentence.

I mixed a fresh tray of print developer as well, though I tend to mix smaller volumes so that I am not tempted to stretch a bath past its useful life. The harbour negative is deceptive: the sky holds more density than the crane, and a hard grade makes a silhouette where a quieter contrast keeps the rigging. I burned the lower dock slightly with a card and then wished I had not. I keep notes in the margin of the contact sheet rather than in a book I will not open. The safelight hum is a useful reminder that the clock is running.

I still test fixer with a scrap of leader, and I still discard it on a schedule rather than on colour. I do not squeegee fibre prints. I hang them from a corner and accept the drip. A torn corner is a worse defect than a drying mark I can later flatten. When the tram passes I pause the exposure. The column is not as rigid as the manufacturer claimed, and I would rather lose a minute than gain a double image I will spend an evening denying.

I prepared a modest volume of print developer and discarded the previous bath, which had remained in use slightly longer than is prudent. The harbour negative continues to resist a single contrast grade: the sky and the crane occupy different regions of the characteristic curve, and a compromise grade leaves one or the other unconvincing. I therefore made two strips and selected the quieter of the two, then applied a small burn to the dock. Whether that burn was necessary remains, I think, open.

Fixer was tested with leader and replaced on the calendar rather than by inspection alone. Fibre prints were hung without squeegeeing. I pause during tram passages because vibration of the column is not imaginary, even if it is slight. These are small disciplines. They do not make the photograph better in any glamorous sense. They keep the process from accumulating a kind of private folklore that I can no longer defend to myself.
