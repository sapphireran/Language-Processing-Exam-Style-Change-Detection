I always play the Italian because I understand the Italian the way I understand toast. You put a knight on f3, you put a bishop on c4, you do not immediately start an adventure you cannot name. If they go for the fried liver I close the laptop and make actual food.

Last night I hung a bishop because I wanted a pretty pawn. It was not pretty. It was a pawn. I sat there grinning at my own mess like it was a personality. 0-1 in twenty. Fine. Toast tomorrow.

The Italian Game (C50–C54) is a 1.e4 e5 opening in which White develops the king's bishop to c4 and typically the king's knight to f3. Its reputation for quietness is historical rather than forced; the Evans Gambit and the Greco Attack remain available. Club players who treat it as toast are describing their repertoire, not the ECO.

A hung piece on move eighteen is not a novelty. It is an unforced error. One may still learn from the pawn structure that survived it, provided the post-mortem does not begin with a joke about food. The fried liver, for the record, is a named attack and not a reason to close the machine.
