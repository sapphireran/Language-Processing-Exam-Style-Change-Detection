I water the Boston fern on Thursdays and I rotate the pot a quarter turn so the fronds do not lean toward the kitchen window.
The soil should feel cool and slightly springy; if the top centimetre is dusty I give it a slow soak from the bottom.
I have learned not to mist the leaves in winter because the droplets sit there and the tips brown overnight.
Last March I moved the plant one metre back from the radiator and the shedding stopped within a fortnight.
The new fronds come out as tight croziers and they unroll only if the room stays above eighteen degrees.
I keep a saucer of pebbles under the pot, not because a magazine told me to, but because the air in this flat is dry enough to crack my knuckles.
When a frond yellows from the base I cut it at the soil line with clean scissors and I do not apologise to the plant.
People talk about ferns as if they were Victorian decoration, but they are just a humidity habit you can keep beside a kettle.
I write the watering dates on a scrap of envelope and I tuck it under the saucer so I cannot pretend I forgot.
If I ever leave for two weeks I ask a neighbour to use the same kettle-and-saucer routine rather than invent a new kindness.
