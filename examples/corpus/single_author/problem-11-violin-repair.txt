The cello that came in on Tuesday has a wolf that everyone has already tried to charm with a brass eliminator clamped in the wrong place. I took it off. The real issue is a soundpost that was cut like a suggestion rather than a column. I fitted a new one, grain oriented the usual way, and the wolf backed down to a rumour. I still don't trust it in a dry hall. I told the player that, which is not what they wanted with a concert on Friday.

I also glued a lifting fingerboard tongue that had been hiding under years of polite rosin. Hide glue, warm, not heroic. Clamped it with the old cork cauls because the fancy ones are somewhere under a heap of chinrests. This workshop is a geography of almost-found tools. If you are reading these notes in a year, the cauls are in the left drawer unless I have lied again.

A cracked rib on the little Saxon violin is waiting for a better day and a better mould. I cleaned the edges. I did not rush a cleat that will show. The customer wanted it "ready for term". Term can wait for a joint that will hold. I wrote that more kindly on the ticket. The ticket is a literary genre. I am not proud of that sentence, and I still left it in the book.

Two bows need rehair before Saturday. I have the horsehair. I do not have the patience if I start them after nine. So I will start them at four and eat later. The yellow lamp makes ivory look ill; switch to daylight before judging a face. I say that every week. I still forget, and then I think a frog has yellowed when it has only been standing under a banana sun.

The kettle is for glue and also for tea, which is a contamination protocol I will not defend. I wiped the bench. I logged hours. I put the wolf-quiet cello on the high shelf so I stop picking at it. Tomorrow I will listen again in the stairwell, which is the only acoustic in this building that tells the truth. If it howls there, it will howl in the hall, and Friday can be someone else's optimism.
