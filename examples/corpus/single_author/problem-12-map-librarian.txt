A reader asked for "the map that shows the ferry before the new slip", which is at least five maps, none of which is called that on the spine. I started with the 1891 harbour plan because it is the one people mean when they want nostalgia with numbers. The slip is a faint pencil ghost. I put the sheet on the large table, not the counter, because elbows have erased more coastlines than the sea. I also fetched the 1924 revision so the argument could have a second date.

I then had to say no to flash photography, which I used to be nicer about. The varnish on the cloth maps blooms like a bruise. I offer a phone shot without flash and a photocopy request form that looks unkind and is actually how we still have a collection. If they sigh, I sigh back. This is still the same desk, the same reader, and the same argument about a coastline.

The coastal series in the rolling stacks has a mislabelled drawer. I keep meaning to type a new card. I have meant this since March. In the meantime I have a sticky note that says "not Shetland, look one left", which is not how a research library is supposed to remember itself. I will type the card on a quiet Wednesday that will not arrive.

Someone returned the town atlas with a croissant flake in the gutter of plate XII. I removed it with a bone folder and a lecture I did not deliver. Food is the hill I will die on. I would rather a reader be slightly offended than a plate be slightly glossy with butter. I wrote "please" on the sign again. The previous please had become furniture.

I spent the last hour tracing a parish boundary that three maps refuse to agree on. That is the job, not a failure of the job. I pencilled the discrepancies on the query slip and offered the reader all three sheets. People want a single line. I sell them a small argument. It is the only honest product.

Closing. I logged the 1891 plan back to its press mark, washed my hands of the cloth dust, and left the sticky note where it is. Tomorrow I will try to be a person who types cards. Tonight I am a person who saved a coastline from an elbow. That is enough for a Tuesday, and I am not taking the atlas home, however much I want to finish the parish fight.
