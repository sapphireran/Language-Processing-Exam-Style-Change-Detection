Wind backed around to the northeast just before dusk and I had to go up again to check the gallery door, which likes to pretend it's latched. The lamp's behaving. I still tap the glass like a superstitious aunt. Below, the harbour is a string of cheap pearls and one bright crane that never sleeps. I wrote the barometer in the book and then wrote it again because the first 8 looked like a 3. That's the quality of my evenings.

The relief keeper is due Thursday if the boat runs. I have enough bread if it does not. I keep telling myself that as if bread were the problem. The problem is the radio drama in my head when the foghorn tests itself at two. I make tea. I do not write letters I will not send. I sharpen the pencil for the log because a dull point makes me sloppy with tenths.

This afternoon a yacht tried to cut inside the red and I stood there with my mouth open like that would help. They corrected. I still logged it. The service likes a paper trail more than it likes my opinions about other people's hulls. Gulls have discovered the compost pail again. I moved it. They will discover the new place. This is not a metaphor. It is a pail.

I oiled the hinge on the trapdoor. I counted the spare mantles. I did not count them twice, which is how you know I am tired. The sea has that dull pewter look that means a quiet night or a lie. I will wind the clock before I sit down. If I sit down first I will listen to the crane and forget. The crane is not my colleague. It only works the same hours.

Dreamed the light went out and I was filling it with kettle water. Woke up annoyed at my own symbolism. The lamp is fine. I walked the stair to prove it, which woke me properly. On the way down I named the steps like I did when I first arrived. It is a childish trick and it still works. Seventeen is the one that complains.

If the boat comes I will send the extra socks back; they are a size that is not mine and I have been too polite. If the boat does not come I will finish the novel that takes place in a city I no longer miss. Either way I will keep the gallery door honest. That is the job when you strip the romance out of it, and I have. I still tap the glass.
