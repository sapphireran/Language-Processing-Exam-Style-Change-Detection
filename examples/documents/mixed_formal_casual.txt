Style change detection asks whether a document remains stylistically homogeneous or whether its local writing habits shift. The operational unit is usually the paragraph. Each unit is mapped to a vector of frequencies, distances between neighbours are computed, and peaks in that series are treated as candidate boundaries.

The appeal of this formulation is that it separates three questions that are often conflated. First, does more than one authorial style appear at all? Second, where do the transitions occur? Third, can paragraphs be grouped into latent authors, including the case in which an earlier style returns after an interruption?

Those questions are not interchangeable. A detector may notice that a document is mixed without placing the boundary on the correct paragraph, and it may place boundaries correctly while still failing to recognise that the first and last blocks belong to the same writer.
