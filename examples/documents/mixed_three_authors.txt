A formal register is characterised by restraint. Sentences are permitted to run long; connectives such as however and therefore are used to mark logical relations; contractions are avoided. The writer does not address the reader as you, nor does the writer linger on personal anecdote. The paragraph is treated as a unit of argument rather than as a turn in a conversation.

That restraint has a measurable footprint. Mean sentence length rises, the rate of first-person pronouns falls, and punctuation shifts toward commas and semicolons rather than question marks. None of these cues is individually decisive. Taken together, they describe a region in feature space that casual prose rarely occupies for long.
