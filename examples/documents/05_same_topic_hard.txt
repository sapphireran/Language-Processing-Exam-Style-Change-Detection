We tokenise by greedily merging the most frequent adjacent pairs until the vocabulary budget is spent.
This works well in practice, although rare words may still fragment into awkward pieces.
We like the method because the training loop stays simple and we can inspect the merges by hand.
If a domain shift hits, we just rebuild the table and hope the new pairs look sensible.
One may regard subword segmentation as a constrained compression of the vocabulary under a multinomial model.
The procedure maximises a corpus likelihood rather than a psycholinguistic claim about morphemes.
Rare types are expected to decompose; that outcome is a feature of the objective, not a defect of implementation.
A domain shift is therefore handled by re-estimating the same objective, not by hoping the merges remain interpretable.
