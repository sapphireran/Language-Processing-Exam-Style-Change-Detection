You feed the starter when it smells lonely, not when the clock says so. A spoon of flour, a splash of water, wait until it looks like a sponge that wants to climb out. If it smells like nail polish you were late; throw half away and start again. My mother never measured and the bread still rose.

Hydration is expressed as water mass over flour mass. At 75 percent the dough is extensible enough for an open crumb, provided gluten has been developed by folds at thirty-minute intervals and the bulk ferment is halted around 50 percent volume increase, not by a wall-clock rule.

Don't scare people with percentages. If the dough sticks to your fingers like gum it is too wet; dust the board. If it tears instead of stretching, it is tired or thirsty. You will know after two loaves.

Alveometry is nevertheless unnecessary at home. What is necessary, however, is a consistent flour, a starter whose peak is observed rather than assumed, and a baking stone that has been above 250 C for at least forty minutes. The rest is commentary.
