Preheat the oven to 180 C.
Chop two yellow onions.
Warm a thin film of oil in the pan.
Stir until the edges brown.
Add a pinch of salt.
Toss the lot into the dish.
Bake twenty minutes.
Do not cover the pan.
The Maillard reaction comprises a family of non-enzymatic browning pathways in which reducing sugars condense with free amino groups.
Elevated surface temperature accelerates the formation of melanoidins, which in turn dominate both colour and the more persistent roasted aromas.
One should distinguish this cascade from caramelisation, a sugar-only process that proceeds without amino nitrogen.
The practical consequence for the cook is that moisture must fall low enough for the surface to exceed approximately 140 C, otherwise the reaction remains kinetically suppressed.
Empirical kitchen practice therefore aligns, somewhat accidentally, with a fairly austere piece of food chemistry.
