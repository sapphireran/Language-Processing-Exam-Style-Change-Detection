I leave the flat at seven and walk the bike down the two flights because the stairwell is too tight to ride.
The harbour wind hits as soon as I turn onto the bridge, and I have to stand on the pedals for the first fifty metres.
I still have not found a pair of gloves that keep my fingers useful after the second canal.
Most mornings I count the same three barges and pretend that means I am on time.
If the lights on Torvegade stack against me I cut through the side street with the bakery, even though the cobbles rattle the basket.
I buy the cheaper coffee there only when I have already missed the first seminar slot.
The rest of the ride is quieter, just the click of the chain and whoever is overtaking on the left without a bell.
I lock the bike at the usual rack and only then check whether I remembered the charger.
Nothing about this route is efficient, but I know every crack in the asphalt and that is the point.
I would not trade it for a bus even in November.
