Board game night went off the rails in the best way. We started with a rules explainer that lasted longer than the actual first game, which is a known failure mode. I kept stealing snacks from the communal bowl and pretending I was tracking other people's scores. I wasn't! By midnight someone had invented a house rule that made no sense and we adopted it anyway because it was funny.

Setup time is the hidden cost function of tabletop play. A game whose first twenty minutes are spent punching tokens and reciting exceptions will lose casual players before the interesting decisions appear. Designers who front-load a teaching scenario, even a slightly scripted one, tend to keep groups intact through the first scoring cycle; the remaining friction is social rather than mechanical. Who is allowed to remind whom of a forgotten action remains an under-specified rule of the table.

I packed it in after the third rematch because my brain was soup. Walking home I tried to reconstruct the last turn and immediately invented a cooler version of what had happened. That's fine! Memory's a house rule too. Next week I'm bringing the shorter game, the one that fits in a bag and doesn't require a speech.
