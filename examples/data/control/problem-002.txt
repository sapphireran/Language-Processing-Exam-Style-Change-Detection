Cataloguing a personal reading list as if it were a corpus is a slightly comic exercise, but it makes rereading less accidental. A short note on date, genre, and whether the book was finished is already enough to reveal seasonal biases. Long novels cluster in winter. Essays appear when work is noisy. The list does not need to be public to be useful; it needs to be complete enough that omissions become visible.

The harder discipline is recording abandoned books without turning the catalogue note into a verdict. A stop can mean a mismatch of timing rather than a failure of the text. Tagging those abandoned books separately keeps the finished-book sample from pretending to be the whole of a year's attention. Over a few years the abandoned set is often the more honest portrait of appetite.

Revisit rules help the catalogue stay honest. A book that was left in April can be restarted in October without shame, provided the note says so. The reading list then becomes a map of returns rather than a scoreboard. That, at least, is the intention. Practice is sloppier, and the sloppiness is part of the data.
