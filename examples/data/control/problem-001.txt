I keep a running list of plants that have forgiven me and plants that haven't. The basil on the kitchen sill is currently on the forgiven list, which is optimistic of it. I water when the soil looks sulky, I rotate the pot when I remember, and I pinch the tops because a video told me to. Sometimes I forget for a week and then overcorrect. The plant has opinions about that!

The tomatoes on the balcony are a different negotiation, but I'm still the same kind of distracted gardener. They want more sun than the building is prepared to offer after three in the afternoon. I moved them twice and then admitted the architecture would win. Still, a few fruits came in small and loud-tasting, which is enough to keep me buying compost I don't have space for. Gardening at this scale is mostly stubbornness plus a watering can.

Friends ask for a system and I don't have one. I have a habit of looking at leaves when I make coffee. Yellowing gets a lecture. New growth gets unearned praise. That's the method! If a plant dies I write it down so I don't repeat the exact same neglect, and then I often repeat a close cousin of it anyway.
