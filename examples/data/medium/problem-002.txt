A loaf is a slow argument with flour. I mix until the shaggy dough just comes together, then I walk away because touching it too early makes me overwork it. The dough tells the truth later: if I rushed the rest, the crumb is tight and a little sulky. I score with more confidence than I feel and I still flinch when the first steam hits the glass!

Preferment the night before if the schedule allows; a poolish at equal parts flour and water with a pinch of yeast will lengthen flavor without a sourdough culture. Target 68 percent hydration for a first loaf, rise until the dough is jiggly but not collapsed, then degas only enough to shape. Bake on a preheated stone with steam for the first twelve minutes. Internal temperature near 96 C is a more reliable doneness cue than color alone.
