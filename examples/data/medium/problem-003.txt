Rain turned the towpath into a ribbon of dark clay and I kept walking the canal anyway. The canal smelled like wet iron. A moorhen crossed without asking permission. I had a list of errands in my pocket and I didn't look at it. The point of the canal loop was to let the list get quieter, not to finish it!

The same canal corridor can be read as a linear park, a freight leftover, or a commuting stub, depending on which agency drew the last map. Surface material, lighting intervals, and access points determine whether the towpath functions after dusk. A clay surface that is charming at noon becomes a slip hazard at 18:00 in February; maintenance regimes, not opening-day photographs, decide the afterlife of a waterside canal walk.

I came back from the canal with mud to the ankles and a head that had finally stopped rehearsing emails. There's a bench by the third bridge that lists slightly toward the water. I sat there long enough for the rain to feel ordinary. Then I went home and did one item from the list, which was enough.
