The first cup I remember wasn't good coffee. It was diner coffee in a thick mug that kept the heat long after the flavor had given up. My father drank it black and I copied him because copying felt like a skill. Years later I learned to smell the coffee grounds before the water hit them, and that small pause started to feel like the actual ritual. I still do it, even when I'm late!

Use a 1:16 ratio of coffee to water by mass. Grind medium-coarse, wet the paper filter, and discard the rinse water. Bloom for 30 seconds with twice the coffee weight in water at 96 C, then pour in concentric circles until you reach the target mass. Total brew time should land between 2:45 and 3:15. If the cup is sour and thin, grind finer; if it is bitter and hollow, grind coarser or lower the temperature by three degrees.

I don't weigh anything when I'm at my sister's place. She has a kettle that whistles like an argument and a jar of coffee beans with no date on it. The cup is still a cup! Maybe that's the unfashionable opinion: the coffee ritual can survive sloppy numbers. I care about the smell more than I care about the scatter in extraction yield.

Water chemistry matters more than most brew guides admit. If your tap water is very soft, the coffee will taste sharp even at a correct grind. Add a pinch of mineral blend or use a bottled water with moderate hardness. Keep the kettle lid closed between pours so temperature does not collapse. Record the grind setting that worked; memory is a poor instrument for coffee.
